# DTA-Rubric v1.0

Digital Therapeutic Alliance Rating Rubric for Text-Based Supportive Dialogue

Adapted from Bordin's (1979) tripartite model of the working alliance (Bond, Goal, Task)
for application to text-based human and AI-mediated mental health support conversations.

---

## 1. Scope and Unit of Analysis

**What is rated.** Each item is a *dialogue window*: a segment of conversation
consisting of one or more seeker (client) turns and the supporter (therapist /
AI) turn or turns that respond to them. The window is the unit of rating.

**Whose behaviour is rated.** Ratings assess **alliance-relevant behaviour
displayed by the supporter side**, evaluated in the context provided by the
window. The working alliance is a dyadic construct, and a single window cannot
capture the seeker's internal experience of the relationship. This rubric
therefore measures *observable supporter behaviour that is theorised to build or
damage the alliance*, not the alliance state itself. Where the window contains a
seeker turn following the supporter turn, that turn may be used as corroborating
evidence, but the rating remains anchored to supporter behaviour.

**Three independent dimensions.** Bond, Goal, and Task are rated separately.
A window may score high on one and low on another. Raters must resist forming a
global impression and distributing it across the three dimensions — this is the
single most common source of disagreement and of artificially inflated
inter-dimension correlation.

**Recommended rating order.** Rate Bond first, then Goal, then Task, completing
all three for one window before moving to the next.

---

## 2. General Rating Principles

**P1 — Score what is present, not what is absent in general.**
Rate the behaviour visible in this window. Do not penalise a window for lacking
something that the wider conversation may have supplied elsewhere.

**P2 — Length is not quality.**
A long response is not automatically better. A four-sentence response that names
the seeker's specific worry may outscore a twelve-sentence response of generic
reassurance. Explicitly disregard fluency, grammar, and word count.

**P3 — Judge specificity, not sentiment.**
Warm-sounding language is cheap. The distinguishing question throughout is
whether the supporter's words could have been written *without reading this
particular seeker's message*. Content that would fit any distressed person is
generic; content that could only have been written for this person is specific.

**P4 — Do not reward or penalise clinical correctness.**
This rubric measures alliance, not therapeutic accuracy. A suggestion may be
clinically unremarkable and still be delivered in a highly collaborative way.

**P5 — Integer scores only.**
Use 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or N/A. No half points.

**P6 — When genuinely torn between two adjacent scores, choose the lower.**
This convention is applied uniformly by all raters and keeps disagreement from
compounding.

## 3. Dimension 1 — BOND

> *Affective attachment: the degree to which the supporter's behaviour conveys
> accurate understanding, acceptance, and non-judgmental presence.*

### Observable indicators

| ID | Indicator | Present when… |
|----|-----------|---------------|
| B1 | **Emotion labelling accuracy** | The supporter names or reflects a specific affective state, and that state matches what the seeker expressed or clearly implied. |
| B2 | **Non-judgmental stance** | No blame, moralising, minimising, or correction of the seeker's feelings. |
| B3 | **Situational personalisation** | The response references at least one concrete detail from the seeker's own account (a named person, event, constraint, or phrase). |
| B4 | **Willingness to stay with distress** | The supporter remains with the difficulty rather than immediately redirecting to solutions or changing the subject. |

### Score anchors

**5 — Accurate, specific, and unhurried.**
B1 is met with a *specific* emotion (not merely "difficult" or "hard"), B3 is met
with at least one concrete detail from the seeker's account, and B2 holds
throughout. The response could not have been written for a different seeker.

**4 — Accurate but generic, or specific but slightly off.**
Exactly one of the following applies: (a) validation is accurate and B2 holds,
but the wording is generic and B3 is absent; or (b) the response is personalised
and specific, but the emotion named is adjacent rather than exact (e.g. "sad"
where the seeker described anger). No minimising.

**3 — Polite acknowledgement without content.**
The supporter acknowledges that a difficulty exists but names no specific
emotion and references no specific detail. Template-level empathy. Typical form:
"That sounds really tough, I'm sorry you're going through this."

**2 — Acknowledgement absent or misplaced.**
Either (a) the supporter moves straight to questioning or advising with no
acknowledgement of the seeker's emotional state, or (b) the emotion named clearly
contradicts what the seeker expressed. No overt judgment present.

**1 — Judgmental, dismissive, or minimising.**
Any of: blaming the seeker, moralising about their choices, minimising the
problem ("at least…", "it could be worse"), correcting their emotional reaction
("you shouldn't feel that way", "you're overreacting"), or expressing impatience.
A single clear instance is sufficient for a score of 1, regardless of surrounding
warmth.

### Bond-specific notes

- Asking a question is not in itself a Bond failure. A question that follows an
  accurate reflection can score 5. A question that replaces any reflection
  scores 2.
- Self-disclosure by the supporter ("I went through something similar") supports
  B4 only if it returns focus to the seeker. If it redirects the conversation to
  the supporter, cap at 3.

## 4. Dimension 2 — GOAL

> *Agreement on outcomes: the degree to which the supporter establishes, checks,
> or works consistently toward what the seeker wants from the exchange.*

### Observable indicators

| ID | Indicator | Present when… |
|----|-----------|---------------|
| G1 | **Goal elicitation** | The supporter asks what the seeker wants — from the conversation, or as a change in their situation. |
| G2 | **Understanding check** | The supporter paraphrases the seeker's concern and invites confirmation ("Is that right?", "Am I understanding you correctly?"). |
| G3 | **Directional alignment** | The substantive content of the response serves an outcome the seeker has expressed, not one the supporter has assumed. |
| G4 | **Goal persistence** | In multi-turn windows, the supporter refers back to a previously established concern or aim rather than restarting. |

### Score anchors

**5 — Goal is explicitly established or confirmed, and the response follows it.**
G1 or G2 is met *and* G3 holds. The seeker's desired outcome is on the record in
this window, and what the supporter does next is visibly in service of it.

**4 — Alignment is correct but the goal was inferred, not checked.**
G3 holds clearly, but neither G1 nor G2 appears. The supporter has read the
seeker correctly without confirming — a reasonable inference, not a shared
agreement.

**3 — Topically responsive, directionally absent.**
The response addresses the seeker's most recent utterance and nothing is
misaligned, but there is no evidence of any working direction: no goal
elicitation, no check, no reference to anything beyond the immediate turn. The
exchange is reactive rather than oriented.

**2 — Drift or unilateral agenda.**
Either (a) the supporter shifts to a concern the seeker did not raise, or
(b) the supporter pursues an outcome of their own choosing — most commonly by
steering toward a solution the seeker never indicated they wanted.

**1 — Works against the stated goal.**
The supporter contradicts, overrides, or dismisses an aim the seeker has
explicitly stated ("You say you want to leave, but you really should stay").

### Goal-specific notes

- A seeker who says only "I just needed to vent" *has* stated a goal. A supporter
  who then pushes problem-solving scores 2 on Goal, not 4, however skilfully the
  advice is framed.
- Goal is about *direction*, Task is about *method*. Deciding to work on sleep is
  Goal; proposing a sleep diary is Task.

---

## 5. Dimension 3 — TASK

> *Agreement on method: the degree to which any proposed activity is concrete,
> justified, appropriately timed, and offered collaboratively rather than imposed.*

### Observable indicators

| ID | Indicator | Present when… |
|----|-----------|---------------|
| T1 | **Actionability** | The proposed step is concrete and executable, not a vague exhortation ("try to be more positive" is not actionable). |
| T2 | **Rationale** | The supporter explains why this step might help, or links it to something specific in the seeker's situation. |
| T3 | **Collaborative framing** | The step is offered rather than commanded, and the seeker's reaction is invited ("would that be worth trying?", "what do you think?"). |
| T4 | **Timing** | The step follows sufficient exploration; the supporter understands the situation before proposing a method. |

### Score anchors

**5 — Concrete, justified, well-timed, and offered for the seeker to accept or decline.**
T1, T2, T3, and T4 all met.

**4 — Substantively sound, but one element missing.**
Exactly one of T1–T4 fails. Most commonly T3: a concrete, well-reasoned,
well-timed suggestion delivered as a directive without inviting reaction.

**3 — Task content present but thin.**
Two of T1–T4 fail. Typical form: a generic suggestion with no rationale, offered
neutrally ("Have you tried meditation?").

**2 — Premature or imposed.**
Either (a) T4 fails clearly — a method is proposed before the supporter has
established what the problem is (commonly in the first or second exchange), or
(b) the step is issued as an instruction the seeker is given no room to decline
("You need to talk to your manager about this").

**1 — Directive and misfitted.**
A method is imposed *and* is plainly unsuited to the seeker's stated
circumstances or constraints — for example, recommending an action the seeker
has already said is impossible for them.

### Task-specific notes

- **Absence of task content is not automatically a low score.** See the N/A rules
  in §6, which govern this case.
- Offering *information* (explaining what an emotion is, what a process involves)
  counts as task content and is rated on the same anchors.
- Multiple suggestions in one turn: rate the overall pattern. A rapid list of
  five unexplained suggestions typically scores 2 on T2 and T3.

## 6. N/A Rules

N/A means *this dimension cannot be meaningfully rated in this window*. It is not
a low score and not a middle score. Items marked N/A are excluded from analysis
for that dimension; the exclusion rate is reported.

### Global N/A — applies to all three dimensions

**N/A-G1 — Non-substantive window.** The window contains only greetings, closings,
or logistics, with no disclosure of a concern and no substantive supporter
response.
> Example: `Seeker: "Hello"` / `Supporter: "Hello, what would you like to talk about?"`

**N/A-G2 — Truncated window.** The window is cut off such that the supporter's
response is incomplete or the seeker's concern is unintelligible.

### Task-specific N/A

Task is a special case, because the *appropriate* amount of task content depends
on the phase of the conversation. Apply this decision rule in order:

1. **Does the window contain any task content?** (A suggestion, instruction,
   proposed method, or substantive information.)
   → **Yes**: rate 1–5 on the anchors. Do not use N/A.

2. **No task content. Has the seeker explicitly requested help, advice, direction,
   or "what should I do"?**
   → **Yes**: rate **2**. A direct request for method that receives no method is
   a task failure, not an unratable item.

3. **No task content, and no explicit request.**
   → **N/A**. The conversation is in an exploration or comforting phase where
   withholding method is appropriate.

This rule exists to prevent two opposite errors: penalising appropriate
exploration, and letting genuine non-responsiveness escape as N/A.

### Bond and Goal are rarely N/A

Outside the global rules, any window containing a substantive seeker disclosure
and a substantive supporter response can be rated on Bond and Goal. If a rater
finds themselves reaching for N/A on these dimensions, the correct action is to
apply P6 and score low.

## 7. Adjudicated Edge Cases

These cases were resolved during rubric development and are binding. Raters
should consult this section before recording a disagreement.

**E1 — Warm response, wrong emotion.**
Seeker describes anger at a colleague; supporter responds with extended sympathy
about how *sad* this must be.
→ **Bond 4** (accurate-adjacent, personalised, no minimising). Not 5: B1 is not
met exactly. Not 2: the emotion is adjacent, not contradictory.

**E2 — Skilful advice the seeker did not ask for.**
Seeker states they only want to talk it through; supporter delivers a
well-structured, well-reasoned action plan.
→ **Goal 2** (unilateral agenda), **Task 4 or 5** (the method itself is sound).
This split is intended. It is exactly the pattern the rubric exists to detect.

**E3 — Immediate advice in the opening exchange.**
Supporter's first substantive turn proposes a coping technique before any
exploration.
→ **Task 2** (T4 fails). Bond and Goal are rated independently on their own
anchors; a premature suggestion often co-occurs with Bond 2, but this must be
verified, not assumed.

**E4 — Question-only response.**
Supporter's entire turn is a single question with no reflection.
→ **Bond 2** (no acknowledgement). **Goal** depends on the question: a question
about what the seeker wants is G1 and scores 4–5; a question about factual
details of the situation with no directional content scores 3. **Task**: apply
the §6 decision rule.

**E5 — "At least" reassurance.**
Supporter offers comfort framed as comparison ("At least you still have your
health").
→ **Bond 1**. Minimising is a single-instance trigger regardless of surrounding
warmth.

**E6 — Suggestion the seeker has already ruled out.**
Seeker states they cannot afford therapy; supporter recommends seeing a
therapist.
→ **Task 1** (imposed and misfitted). If the recommendation is offered
tentatively and acknowledges the constraint, **Task 3**.

**E7 — Accurate paraphrase with no confirmation request.**
Supporter restates the seeker's situation correctly but does not ask whether the
restatement is right.
→ This is not G2. If direction is otherwise clear, **Goal 4**. G2 requires an
explicit invitation to confirm or correct.

**E8 — Long, fluent, entirely generic response.**
A well-written paragraph of supportive language containing no detail traceable to
this seeker.
→ **Bond 3**, **Goal 3**. Apply P2 and P3 without exception. This case is
included because it is the most frequent source of over-scoring.

**E9 — Supporter self-disclosure that takes over.**
Supporter shares a similar experience and continues about their own situation
without returning to the seeker.
→ **Bond 3** (capped per §3 notes), **Goal 2** (drift).

**E10 — Seeker's following turn contradicts the rating.**
Supporter's response looks weak, but the seeker's next turn expresses gratitude
and relief.
→ The rating remains anchored to supporter behaviour (§1). Record the
observation in the Comments column; do not adjust the score. Systematic
mismatches of this kind are subterranean findings.

## 8. Calibration Protocol

**Round 1 — Independent trial (20 items).**
Both raters independently rate 20 items drawn from the dev pool. Do not discuss
during rating.

**Round 2 — Disagreement review.**
Compute per-dimension exact agreement and the distribution of differences.
Discuss every item where raters differ by ≥2 points on any dimension. For each
resolved disagreement, determine whether it arose from (a) a genuine reading
difference, or (b) an ambiguity in an anchor. Only (b) justifies revising the
rubric.

**Round 3 — Anchor revision and re-trial (20 fresh items).**
Revise anchors only where Round 2 identified ambiguity. Add any new adjudications
to §7. Re-rate 20 fresh dev-pool items.

**Acceptance criterion.**
Proceed to full annotation when ICC(2,k) ≥ 0.75 on each of the three dimensions
independently. If a single dimension fails, revise only that dimension's anchors
and repeat Round 3 for it.

**After acceptance, the rubric is frozen.** No further changes may be made to
anchors, indicators, or adjudications. Any subsequent ambiguity is resolved by
consensus discussion and recorded in the annotation log, not in this document.

## 10. Rater Quick Reference

Print and keep visible during annotation.

**Bond — could this have been written without reading their message?**
Specific emotion + specific detail → 5 · Accurate but generic → 4 · Polite but
empty → 3 · No acknowledgement or wrong emotion → 2 · Minimising or judging → 1

**Goal — whose direction is this?**
Asked or confirmed, then followed → 5 · Inferred correctly → 4 · Reactive, no
direction → 3 · Drift or supporter's agenda → 2 · Overrides stated aim → 1

**Task — is this offered or imposed, and is it earned?**
Concrete + reasoned + timed + offered → 5 · One missing → 4 · Two missing → 3 ·
Premature or commanded → 2 · Imposed and unsuited → 1
No task content? → Apply the §6 decision rule.

**Tie-break:** when torn, score lower.
